Miren Vale

Dark fantasy, folk horror, and ghost-soaked fiction shaped by inheritance, ritual, and the unseen.

Stories concerned with thresholds, buried knowledge, and what follows once the old warnings are ignored.

The work is concerned with thresholds: the places people are warned not to enter, the knowledge families try to bury, and the moment an ordinary landscape begins to reveal what has always been present beneath it.

Miren Vale writes dark fantasy and folk horror rooted in isolated communities, old beliefs, ruined places, and the consequences of answering what should have been ignored.

The stories often begin with something small and physical: a sealed letter, a mark on the skin, a road missing from the map, or a voice heard where no one is standing. From there, they move into questions of inheritance, complicity, and the cost of being noticed.

The Crossing follows a young woman drawn towards a coastal trial after finding an invitation hidden inside an unmarked library book. Its world is built from drowned paths, ruined chapels, family silence, and rituals that refuse to remain buried.

These stories do not offer safety through disbelief.

Once the threshold has been crossed, something always follows.
